A neuromanifold is the space of functions that a neural network computes as its weights vary. Below is the neuromanifold of a tiny residual network, with two layers of one neuron each, no biases, and a polynomial activation σ. It computes

f(x) = σ(w2(σ(w1x) + x)) + σ(w1x) + x,

so as the weights (w1, w2) vary, f traces a surface in the space of polynomials of degree deg(σ)2.

Choose σ, and see the surface through a 3D projection, orthogonal for the Bombieri–Weyl inner product: a random one, or its top three principal components (PCA). You can rotate the projection through the hidden dimensions (Shift + drag). Features of the surface itself, such as its singularities, are there in every projection; artifacts of a projection move around as it rotates. You can also train the network: click on the disk of weights to run gradient descent from there, on the squared Bombieri–Weyl distance to a target ◆ (a random function of the network, plus noise), optionally regularized by the squared norm of the weights, and follow its path on the disk and on the surface.
