A neuromanifold is the space of functions that a neural network computes as its weights vary. Below is the neuromanifold of a tiny residual network, with two layers of one neuron each, no biases, and a polynomial activation σ. It computes

f(x) = σ(w2(σ(w1x) + x)) + σ(w1x) + x,

so as the weights (w1, w2) vary, f traces a surface in the space of polynomials of degree deg(σ)2. Choose σ, and see the surface through a 3D projection, orthogonal for the Bombieri–Weyl inner product: a random one, or its top three principal components (PCA). You can rotate the projection through the hidden dimensions. Features of the surface itself, such as its singularities, are there in every projection; artifacts of a projection move around as it rotates.

Point at the disk to mark the function f of those weights in the view (faintly where the surface hides it). Click to train the network from there.

 

Training is gradient descent on the squared Bombieri–Weyl distance to a target ◆: a random function of the network, plus noise.

 

The surface is colored by the weights (w1, w2), as in the disk: hue for their direction and saturation for their size. The dark grid lines are the weight axes w1 = 0 and w2 = 0. When σ is even, the weights (±w1, ±w2) all give the same function, so they get the same color, and the colors repeat in the four quadrants of the disk.
